Review Conclave · one 16-PR feature stack · October 2026

Three reviewers, one stack, and what each one caught

We ran a review Conclave over a 16-PR stack: three AI reviewers from three vendors on every PR, a gate from a fourth, then one more pass over the whole stack at once. 114 findings came out. 61 were posted. Here is who found what, and what reviewing the whole stack added on top of reviewing each PR.

Our brief did most of the finding

Fable on our written brief found 99 of 114 and was the only source of 40 of the 61 posted. A follow-up put Opus and Astra on the same brief: most of that lead came from the brief, not the model.

The reviewers barely overlap

Any two of them share about a fifth of what they find. Dropping one loses its findings, so overlap alone is no reason to cut a seat.

The whole stack earns a small, cheap pass

One Conclave over the whole stack, about 7% of review time, found the one bug no PR review could see and pushed a held bug past the gate. A check at the top of the stack kept 11 already-fixed findings from posting.

How a Conclave works

Three vendors look, a fourth decides what is worth saying, a person decides what goes out

  1. Brief. The chair writes what to look for: the ticket, the risks, the rubric.
  2. Three reviewers, in parallel. Each gets its own copy of the code and cannot see the others.
  3. Blind. Reports are shuffled into A, B, C with model names removed.
  4. Merge. The chair turns three reports into one findings list. It does not vote and gives no lean.
  5. Gate. Jev, a classifier from a fourth company, marks each finding post, hold or second look.
  6. Decide. A person reads the gated list and decides what is posted, and how.
Separate the roles by vendorThe vendor that built the code or chairs the review does not vote on it. That keeps anyone from grading their own work.
Blinding is not enoughOn 39 past blinded reports, report format alone named the vendor 35 times. Separation does the work blinding can't.
For a stack, two extra stepsAfter the 16 per-PR Conclaves: a check of every finding against the top of the stack, then one Conclave on the whole stack as a single diff.
From 114 findings to 16 reviews

Most findings were posted, but in far fewer pieces

Every finding that passed the gate came with a test that reproduces it, and 57 of the 58 kept were distinct defects. So the cut changed how they were delivered, not how many: one review per PR, high and medium severity as line comments, low severity as a checklist in the summary.

The three reviewers

On the big run: Fable finds the most, Astra is the most precise

Minutes per posted finding comes out about equal: Fable spends more time and finds proportionally more. Severity mix is also similar, about half high. Fable on PRs 11 to 16 ran through Cursor at extra-high effort after the weekly allowance on Max ran low; it found about one more per PR there but posted no more, and those PRs were also larger.

The whole versus the sum of its parts

The stack pass found less, but found what the parts could not

The per-PR Conclaves produced 102 findings. One more Conclave over the whole stack, all 11,990 lines as one diff, produced 12. That pass was never going to replace the parts. The question is what it added that they could not.

What the whole added

Connections: bugs that only exist when two PRs meet, like a value one PR stores and a later PR never reads. Confidence: one bug the PR-level gate held cleared it once the reviewers could see the code on both sides. Clean-up: the check at the top of the stack removed 11 findings that a later PR had fixed and showed that 7 PR-level findings were the same bug, posted once on the PR that introduced it.

What it cost

The stack ballot took 0.38 of the run's 5.66 reviewer-hours, about 7%, and 12 minutes of wall time with the three reviewers in parallel. It produced 2 of the 61 posted findings that the parts would not have posted on their own, plus 4 cross-PR leads still waiting on a person. For one extra Conclave, that is a good trade. It is no substitute for the PR-level Conclaves, which found 102.

The brief doubles a model's output

On our brief, Opus found twice what its built-in review found, and Astra two-thirds more. Brief and built-in share only about a fifth of findings for the same model.

Fable still leads, by less

On the same brief, Fable passed 21 to Opus's 14. On the big run the gap was about 3 to 1; here it is 1.4 to 1. Fable and Opus on the brief overlap 65%, and Opus found nothing Fable missed.

Built-in reviews still add a little

Opus built-in found 2 and Astra built-in 1 that no reviewer on the brief caught. Opus built-in takes about 9 minutes.

Four PRs only. Astra's built-in numbers on the big run understate it: its shell could not run Ruby on 16 of 17 ballots, so it reviewed without running tests. That is fixed now; the next reviews will show its real numbers.

What this run cannot tell us yet

Three open questions

Were the posted comments right?The real precision is what the author fixes, disputes or ignores. That gets checked on October 12.
Does effort matter?Six PRs ran Fable at extra-high instead of high, and they were also the larger PRs. The two can't be separated here.
Does it generalize?One author, one stack, one domain, and a four-PR follow-up. The two earlier, smaller reviews had Opus finding the most.
